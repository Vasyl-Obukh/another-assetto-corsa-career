Damage, fuel, tyre wear and slipstream become bounded sliders with named stops. Each is a multiplier with an obvious "real" point, so the field says what a value means instead of asking for a bare number.
  • Damage: 0 to 100 %, stops Off · Half · Real.
  • Fuel use and tyre wear: ×0 to ×5 in steps of 0.1, stops Off · Real · ×2 · ×3.
  • Slipstream: ×0 to ×10, stops Off · Real · ×3 · ×5.
  • The ranges are meant to match Content Manager's own sliders, so we never offer a value CM would refuse. I'll confirm each one in test mode before building.
  • A stop is a click target. The figure beside the label says the value, and a tick marks Real on the track so you can see how far off it you are.
  • An empty field today means "whatever Assetto Corsa ships". It becomes a Default chip that clears the value, with the slider shown dimmed at that default.
  • Tyre blankets stays a switch.

Race control and "Models needed" become choices with a closed set or a bounded stepper.
  • Wheels allowed off becomes chips: Track's own · 1 · 2 · 3. Today it takes any number, and -1 (the track's own) is something you'd have to know.
  • Jump start penalty becomes chips of seconds, None · 5 s · 10 s · 15 s · 30 s, matching what the shipped championships use (10 and 15).
  • Models needed becomes a stepper bounded from 1 to the number of models the spec holds, with "of 9 models in GT3 (2010s)" beside it. It can't ask for more models than exist.
  • Every stepper in the app now clamps whatever is typed into its limits, and that part is already built.
Open question before I build the jump start control: the shipped championships write JUMP_START_PENALTY as seconds, but Content Manager's Quick Drive may treat that key as a three-way choice (locked in place, back to the pits, or drive-through). I'll run it through test mode first. If it turns out to be the three-way choice, the chips become those three, and the shipped championships' values need correcting.
